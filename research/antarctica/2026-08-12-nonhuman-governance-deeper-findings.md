# Non-Human Governance — Deeper Findings

Status: JPV Labs verification note

## Finding 1 — Antarctica is unusually inspection-oriented, not legally opaque

The Antarctic Treaty creates an inspection regime that cuts directly against the common claim that the continent is governed through legally protected secrecy. Consultative Parties may designate observers who have complete freedom of access at any time to any or all areas of Antarctica, including stations, installations, equipment, and ships or aircraft at points where personnel or cargo embark or disembark. Parties must also exchange advance information concerning expeditions, stations, and military personnel/equipment introduced into Antarctica.

Primary sources:
- Antarctic Treaty Secretariat, Peaceful Use and Inspections: https://www.ats.aq/e/peaceful.html
- Antarctic Treaty Secretariat, Exchange Requirements: https://www.ats.aq/e/information.html
- Antarctic Treaty Secretariat, Treaty text summary: https://www.ats.aq/e/antarctictreaty.html

Implication: a claim of a large, persistent, treaty-area non-human governing infrastructure would need to explain how that infrastructure survives a multinational inspection and information-exchange system. This does not logically exclude concealment, but it substantially raises the evidentiary burden.

## Finding 2 — Antarctica's real governance bottleneck is consensus among human state parties

Twenty-nine Consultative Parties currently participate in Antarctic Treaty decision-making. Measures, Decisions, and Resolutions are adopted by consensus; legally binding Measures require approval by all Consultative Parties. The institutional bottleneck is therefore human interstate consensus, not a verified external actor.

Primary sources:
- Antarctic Treaty Secretariat, Parties: https://www.ats.aq/devAS/Parties?lang=en
- Antarctic Treaty Secretariat, ATCM and Other Meetings: https://www.ats.aq/e/atcm.html

Implication: if a source claims that "something else" actually governs Antarctica, the test is not rhetorical. It must identify where the verified consensus mechanism is bypassed, captured, coerced, or made irrelevant, and produce evidence for that mechanism.

## Finding 3 — The `resources` angle has a real legal structure

Antarctic mineral-resource activity is prohibited except for scientific research. The Environmental Protocol has no expiration date, and the 2048 claim is explicitly identified by the Treaty Secretariat as a public misconception. Any future removal of the mineral-resource prohibition would require a binding legal regime and consensus conditions.

Primary sources:
- Antarctic Treaty Secretariat, Environmental Protocol: https://www.ats.aq/e/protocol.html
- Antarctic Treaty Secretariat, Resolution 3 (2023): https://www.ats.aq/devAS/Meetings/Measure/807

Implication: the strongest resource-governance hypothesis is currently about human legal authority, treaty design, future strategic incentives, logistics, research access, fisheries, and geopolitical positioning—not verified hidden extraction by non-human actors.

## Finding 4 — "Unresolved" UAP evidence cannot be promoted to "non-human"

AARO's current official position remains that the Department has found no evidence of extraterrestrial technology. AARO also publishes unresolved cases, including cases where a physical object is likely present but data are insufficient for conclusive attribution. That distinction matters: `UNRESOLVED` is an epistemic state, not an origin classification.

Primary sources:
- AARO FAQ/Home: https://www.aaro.mil/
- AARO official UAP imagery and case records: https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/
- AARO Congressional/Press Products: https://www.aaro.mil/Congressional-Press-Products/

Implication: any chain that converts `unidentified -> anomalous -> non-human -> governing actor` without new evidence is invalid.

## Finding 5 — The strongest verified "non-human governance" mechanism is institutional abstraction

Legal systems explicitly recognize artificial persons. Corporations and other juridical entities can own property, contract, litigate, and persist as entities distinct from any one human. Separately, automated systems already execute consequential routing and market functions, and federal agencies increasingly deploy AI in operational workflows. Regulators and auditors therefore treat automated systems as governance/accountability objects because their failure can affect markets, public programs, privacy, and rights.

Primary sources:
- Cornell LII, artificial person: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/artificial_person
- Cornell LII, legal person: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_person
- SEC, Regulation SCI: https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2015/12/regulation-systems-compliance-integrity
- GAO, AI Accountability Framework: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-21-519sp
- GAO, Federal AI use and management, 2025: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107653

Implication: "the things running the world are not humans" can be literally true in a narrow legal/technical sense without implying extraterrestrial or supernatural beings. Institutions, corporations, algorithms, and protocols can exercise durable decision authority while no single human is the operative actor at every step.

## Root mechanism candidate

The highest-evidence interpretation is not `hidden beings govern humans`.

It is:

`humans create abstractions -> abstractions acquire durable legal/technical authority -> humans increasingly defer decisions to those abstractions -> responsibility fragments across institutions -> outcomes are produced by systems no single human directly authors -> observers experience the resulting authority as non-human or impersonal governance`.

This mechanism is compatible with the broader JPV purpose-mechanism finding: the abstraction begins as a tool, then becomes an authority-bearing mechanism whose preservation can outrank the human purpose it was created to serve.

## Falsification requirements for the literal hidden-being hypothesis

To advance beyond DISCOVERY, require at least one independently authenticated chain demonstrating:

1. an identifiable non-human actor;
2. reliable evidence that the actor exists as claimed;
3. a traceable interface between that actor and a human institution or resource system;
4. evidence of authority, command, coercion, ownership, or decision influence;
5. independent corroboration from a source chain not dependent on the original social-media ecosystem;
6. evidence robust to source-context reconstruction, sensor error, synthetic media, misidentification, and institutional/metaphorical interpretations;
7. for Antarctica-specific claims, an explanation consistent with or evidentially defeating the inspection, information-exchange, treaty-consensus, and research-access record.

Until those conditions are met, the literal claim remains non-adopted.

## Current ranking
